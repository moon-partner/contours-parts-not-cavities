Reconstructing 3D shape from three orthographic silhouettes is deceptively
under-constrained. We make the boundary explicit and build two practical
methods on the observable side of it.

(1) Theorem 1 (identifiability boundary): for any object whose exterior is
axis-visible, the visual-hull error set equals exactly the union of enclosed
cavities -- closed cavities carry zero information in contours, for per-part
or joint methods alike. (2) Semantic part assembly: shared-frame per-part
reconstruction gated by a leave-one-out semantic compatibility table recovers
part-contact topology exactly (35 synthetic objects x 7 conditions; all three
pre-registered criteria pass). (3) Confidence-weighted solid priors: a
hull-gated solid-volume regularizer with warmup fixes the false-cavity
pathology of contour-only SDF fitting (-75.5%, p=0.039 primary; -72.9%,
p=8e-5 pooled) and reveals an all-solid attractor explosion as a
dose-dependent failure mode. (4) Real-geometry transfer: three ABO product
models, split and voxelized fully automatically, yield exact contact recovery
and 1-2% world-normalized Chamfer with zero-shot semantic transfer.

The deposit contains the paper draft, all experimental code, per-run records,
figures, and an independent verification suite (98 assertions, 0 mismatches).
A public GitHub repository provides one-command reproduction (~3 minutes,
cached results); see related identifiers.
